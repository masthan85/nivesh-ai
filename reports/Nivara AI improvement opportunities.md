# Nivara AI should earn trust before it scales

Nivara AI (formerly Nivesh AI) has a sound idea and a candid set of design documents, but the code is an early prototype that cannot yet support its own central promise of "never fabricate". It is meant to be a broker-neutral workspace where Indian retail investors see all their holdings in one place and ask "Nia", an AI assistant that "explains rather than advises", what those holdings mean (`README.md:3-5`). In practice only one real user journey works end to end (sign in, connect Groww, view a snapshot). The portfolio screens, Nia and markets pages still run on hard-coded demo content. Several verified defects undermine trust directly: a login bug that locks out users who type capital letters, a production secret check that accepts the publicly known template key, and a tax figure of ₹20,000 shown on a net-zero gain. Beyond the code, three external facts shape what the product can become. SEBI judges advice by substance, not labels, so a chatbot that comments on a user's own holdings is close to regulated advice. Account Aggregator data is open only to regulated entities. And any public display of NSE prices, even delayed, needs a licence. The opportunity is still real. Groww, Angel One and INDmoney all now ship AI assistants, but broker assistants see only their own broker's accounts, and India has about 13 crore unique investors of whom only 36% report moderate or high financial knowledge. The recommended path is to fix the trust-critical defects within weeks, then rebuild the portfolio around transactions and licensed, consented data, then turn Nia into a grounded, tool-driven explainer with tested guardrails, and to obtain a written opinion from securities counsel before launch. Nothing in this report is legal advice, and figures marked as unverified need confirming against primary sources or vendor quotes.

## A credible plan sits on top of a thin prototype

The repository describes the product's intended shape well, even though it has no product requirements document, user stories or acceptance criteria; the requirements are implicit across the architecture, security and testing documents. The target is a modular monolith: a React client calling a versioned FastAPI service, with domain modules for portfolio, goals, taxes, broker costs, risk and alerts, PostgreSQL as the system of record, Redis for caching, and async workers for alerts, imports and briefings (`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md:3-5`). Three architectural decisions are good ones. **Money is held as Decimal and NUMERIC throughout, with float allowed only at presentation boundaries** (`docs/ADR-002-FINANCIAL-PRECISION.md:5,13`). **Market data sits behind replaceable adapters, yfinance is a development-only source, and missing data must be shown as unavailable rather than invented** (`docs/ADR-003-MARKET-DATA-PROVIDERS.md:5-11`). **LLMs must never perform financial calculations**, and every datum should carry source, timestamp and freshness. The security model targets OWASP ASVS 5.0 and the OWASP AI security standard, with allowlisted, server-authorised tools and retrieved text treated as untrusted (`docs/SECURITY_MODEL.md:3-7`).

What exists today is much narrower. The backend offers registration, per-user holdings, goals, watchlists and saved alert rules with correct ownership isolation, Decimal calculators for valuation, goals, tax and broker costs, Alembic migrations, and a Nia endpoint that answers from holdings alone (`README.md:9-15`). Alerts are saved but never evaluated, broker rate cards cover five brokers and are labelled unverified, and tax output is described as hypothetical (`README.md:19-21`). The production-readiness tracker, dated 26 September 2026, lists backups as not implemented, data licensing and production deployment as blocked, and gives a release decision of "not production-ready" (`docs/PRODUCTION_READINESS_TRACKER.md:5-27`). Of the nine workspaces the tracker names as product scope (Risk, Goals, Tax, Broker Costs, What-if, News, Alerts, Connections, Settings), only Connections exists as a user interface.

The documentation is a better guide to intent than to state. The README says the frontend is "not yet connected to the backend" (`README.md:17`), yet since the last commit the app runs real sign-in, registration and Groww snapshot calls (`frontend/src/App.jsx:47-155`), which a later README section admits (`README.md:79-87`). Meanwhile the holdings shown are hard-coded (`App.jsx:11-16`), Nia in the browser is keyword-matched and never calls the backend's `/ai/chat` route (`App.jsx:64-73`), and the portfolio and market API modules are not imported anywhere. Three "verified" claims in the 30 September capability assessment no longer hold at HEAD: **frontend lint fails with two errors, `npm audit` reports three high-severity advisories (in vite and launch-editor, affecting the dev server rather than the production bundle), and the test count has moved from 26 to 30** (`docs/CAPABILITY_ASSESSMENT.md:81,85`). The last commit evidently did not pass the CI it describes, because both the lint and audit steps (`.github/workflows/ci.yml:68,70`) would fail.

## Five verified defects matter more than any new feature

The earlier stabilisation pass fixed the worst problems, such as wildcard CORS with credentials and fabricated seeded holdings, and authorisation scoping on create, read, update and delete routes is consistently correct. The remaining defects fall into three groups: those that break trust outright, those that weaken security, and those that will fail under real load.

**Authentication.** The most damaging bug is in email handling, and it was reproduced. Pydantic's `EmailStr` lowercases only the domain, so `Mixed@Example.COM` is stored as `Mixed@example.com` (`backend/app/schemas/user.py:23`), while login looks up the raw typed value (`backend/app/routers/auth.py:42`). A user who registers with capitals is then rejected both when typing the address as entered and when typing it in lowercase, and a second, case-variant account can be registered for the same person. The second verified defect is the production secret check, which rejects only keys under 32 characters or the literal "change-this-in-production" (`backend/app/config.py:43`). The value in `.env.example` is 47 characters, so it passes. **Anyone who deploys from the template without changing the key ships a publicly known HS256 signing key, which allows full token forgery.** Around these sit weaker controls: the JWT is kept in `localStorage` (`frontend/src/api/client.js:12-13`) while nginx sets no Content-Security-Policy or HSTS (`frontend/nginx.conf:6-8`); tokens have no `jti`, audience or issuer and cannot be revoked (`backend/app/utils/auth.py:31-34`); there is no per-account login throttle; and registration reveals whether an email exists.

**Rate limiting, logging and resilience.** The rate limiter keys on `request.client.host` (`backend/app/utils/request_context.py:33`), so behind a proxy every user shares one bucket, and its per-IP dictionary is never pruned. The limit is a single global 120 requests a minute per process, with nothing tighter for login, Groww or Nia. The client's `X-Request-ID` header is accepted at any length (a 5,000-character value was echoed back in testing) and written to a 64-character audit column (`backend/app/models/audit.py:13`); on PostgreSQL this would plausibly turn a login into a server error, although that was inferred rather than executed. Audit coverage stops at registration, login and Groww connection, so profile changes, holding and goal edits, and Nia calls leave no trace. The Groww client crashes with a 500 if the broker returns a non-object JSON body (`backend/app/services/broker_connections.py:70-79`), and the Motilal login URL exposes the API key to the browser with no `state` parameter or callback route (`broker_connections.py:54-58`). The price cache in `backend/app/utils/cache.py` is dead code, so **every holdings, summary, tax, briefing and watchlist request makes one blocking yfinance call per holding** inside synchronous handlers, which will exhaust FastAPI's thread pool (about 40 threads by default) under modest load. The provider also ignores the holding's stored exchange and stamps `as_of` with fetch time rather than exchange time, which overstates how fresh the data is.

**Tax and charges.** These matter most for a product that promises not to mislead. The headline rates are correct for listed equity after the July 2024 Budget: 20% STCG, 12.5% LTCG above a ₹1.25 lakh exemption ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/budget-2024-hikes-ltcg-tax-rate-to-12-5-stcg-to-20-stt-on-f-o-also-up-124072300553_1.html)). But **the calculator ignores loss set-off**: a probe with ₹1,00,000 of short-term gains and ₹1,00,000 of short-term losses returned a total tax of ₹20,000 (`backend/app/services/tax_calculator.py:56-60`). It also omits the 4% health and education cess that worked examples apply on top of LTCG ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1052279)), silently treats a missing buy date as 400 days old (which classifies the holding as long-term and picks the lower rate), and taxes every ETF as equity even where the underlying is gold, debt or overseas. It references neither the Income-tax Act 1961 nor its successor, the Income-tax Act 2025, which renumbers the LTCG provision from 1 April 2026 ([Bajaj Finserv](https://www.bajajfinserv.in/investments/section-112a-income-tax-act)). The broker-charge engine is out of date for derivatives. **From 1 April 2026, STT on futures is 0.05% and on options 0.15% of premium, both on the sell side** ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/markets-fm-hikes-stt-on-fo-trades-surprise-move-126020100888_1.html); [ICICI Direct](https://www.icicidirect.com/ilearn/futures-and-options/articles/stt-changes-in-budget-2026-what-f-o-traders-should-know)). The code applies one 0.05% "F&O" rate to notional value and has no premium input, so options cannot be modelled at all (`backend/app/services/broker_charges.py:34`). It ignores trade side, charging sell-only STT and DP fees together with buy-only stamp duty on a single leg. It uses a flat 0.00325% exchange charge for every segment when NSE cash moved to 0.00297% in October 2024 ([Zerodha bulletin](https://zerodha.com/marketintel/bulletin/391488/revision-in-transactions-charges-from-1st-october-2024)); Zerodha now shows 0.00307% ([Zerodha support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/account-opening/resident-individual/ri-charges/articles/exchange-transaction-charges)), but the effective date of that figure was not found. It also leaves SEBI fees out of the 18% GST base. All five rate cards show zero delivery brokerage, which was not checked against current broker tariffs and should be treated as unverified.

**Precision and the domain model.** Decimal precision is kept internally but lost at the API boundary, because `as_number` returns a float for every response (`backend/app/domain/money.py:24-26`) and mutual fund units with eight decimal places are emitted as floats. The deeper problem is structural. **Each holding stores one average price and a free-text buy date, with no transactions, lots or corporate actions** (`backend/app/models/portfolio.py:14-19`). That makes XIRR, FIFO tax lots, realised gains and broker reconciliation impossible. Every serious feature on the roadmap depends on replacing this model.

**Testing and CI.** The backend suite passes (30 tests in about 6 seconds on SQLite), but it is heavy on API journeys and light on financial correctness. Nothing tests email case, loss set-off, cess, the 365-day boundary, statutory charges against published examples, rate limiting or Groww error paths. There are no frontend unit tests and no browser end-to-end tests. The CI design is reasonable for a prototype: it covers SQLite and PostgreSQL, runs a migration round trip with drift check, and includes pip-audit and gitleaks. It lacks Python linting and type checking, coverage gates, static analysis, container builds and scans, SHA-pinned actions, a dependency-update bot and any staging deployment. Because a high-severity `npm audit` blocks the job, new dev-dependency advisories will keep breaking the build until updates are automated.

## Regulation fixes the product's outer boundary

*This section summarises public secondary sources and is not legal advice. Several SEBI circulars could not be read in full during research, and Nivara AI should obtain a written opinion from Indian securities and data-protection counsel before launch.*

**SEBI's adviser rules turn on substance, which puts Nia's core behaviour close to the line.** Anyone who gives advice on specific securities for consideration, or holds out as an adviser "by whatever name called", must register as an Investment Adviser ([Nishith Desai Associates](https://www.nishithdesai.com/default.aspx?id=5966)). The only clear exemptions cover good-faith general comments that name no particular security, and advice through mass media widely available to the public ([IA Regulations text, ICMAI copy](https://icmai.in/upload/pd/SEBI-IA-Regulations-2013.pdf)). A chat assistant that reads one person's holdings and comments on them fits neither exemption well. Law-firm commentary is explicit that calling buy or sell signals "educational" does not avoid registration ([Aarna Law](https://www.aarnalaw.com/insights/sebis-new-digital-compliance-rules-what-investment-advisers-must-know-in-2026)). The README states that Nivara is not registered and that its position "has not yet been reviewed" (`README.md:71-73`). On the evidence gathered, factual aggregation of a user's own data, deterministic analytics (allocation, concentration, XIRR, cost and tax illustrations) and generic explanation of concepts are the lower-risk zone. Output that says or implies "buy, sell, hold, switch or rebalance this" for a specific user looks like advice. Views, ratings or target prices on listed securities look like research analyst activity. These boundaries are inferences that counsel should test.

The December 2024 amendments, notified on 16 December 2024, matter for any future registration. They created part-time adviser and analyst categories (part-time advisers are capped at 75 clients), raised the individual adviser threshold from 150 to 300 clients ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/sebi-eases-norms-for-boarding-of-investment-advisors-research-analysts-124121701008_1.html)), and **require registered advisers and analysts to disclose their use of AI tools and make them solely responsible for AI-generated output, whether built or bought** ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2024/12/19/sebi-research-analyst-third-amendment-regulations-2024/); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/sebi/sebi-eases-rules-investment-advisers-research-analysts-2024-overhaul.html)). A fee cap of ₹1,51,000 a year per family, or 2.5% of assets under advice, was reported from an October 2025 circular but needs checking against the current master circular ([ELP Law](https://elplaw.in/leadership/sebi-permits-investment-advisers-to-charge-aua-based-fees-for-a-second-opinion/)). If Nivara ever wants personalised guidance, the realistic routes are its own non-individual registration, a part-time registration for a founder-led pilot, or operating under a registered partner who signs off on output.

**The finfluencer rules make Nivara's distribution partners part of its compliance story.** Since August 2024, SEBI-regulated entities may have no direct or indirect association with anyone giving unregistered advice or making return claims ([Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/sebi-amends-intermediaries-regulations-bars-intermediaries-from-associating-with-unregistered-security-advisors)). The January 2025 circular adds that **education providers may use only price data at least three months old and must not use stock names or codes in ways that suggest advice** ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/sebi-finfluencer-circular-live-stock-data-market-education-rules-125013000571_1.html)). Showing users their own holdings at live prices is portfolio reporting rather than education, but Nia commenting on named stocks at current prices sits near the conduct SEBI is targeting. The commercial consequence is that Groww, Motilal Oswal, AMCs and depository participants, all of them regulated, have reason to refuse or withdraw API access from an app whose output looks like unregistered advice. SEBI's AI/ML guidelines remain a June 2025 consultation proposing that vendor AI is deemed the regulated entity's own, with accountable senior management, explainability, human review and segregated pre-deployment testing ([SEBI consultation paper](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/jun-2025/1750415065695.pdf)). In November 2026 the SEBI Chairman said final guidelines would come "soon", focused on disclosure, accountability and human oversight ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-sebi-ai-disclosure-human-oversight-rules/)); no final circular was found. Building to those proposals now costs little and will be expected by any partner.

**The retail algo framework leaves a read-only app alone, provided it stays read-only.** SEBI's February 2025 circular ends open broker APIs and requires vendor-client API keys, whitelisted static IPs and exchange registration of algos, with full application from 1 April 2026 under a later glide-path circular ([CSE copy of circular](https://www.cse-india.com/upload/upload/Feb_042025.pdf); [AlgoBulls](https://algobulls.com/blog/industry-insights-and-updates/sebi-new-algotrading-regulations-for-retail-investors-2026)). It targets order placement. **Nivara should never add an order button**, which would draw it into vendor empanelment and strengthen the case that it advises. Separately, retail API keys are licensed for the account holder's own use, so a multi-user app needs formal partner agreements with each broker.

**Account Aggregator is the best holdings channel but is closed to an unregistered app.** Under the RBI framework, a Financial Information User must be regulated by a financial-sector regulator and use data within its licensed purpose ([NSDL FIP page](https://nsdl.com/resources/fip); [Sahamati FAQ](https://sahamati.org.in/faq/)). CDSL has shared equities, mutual fund units and ETFs as a provider since April 2023 ([CDSL](https://www.cdslindia.com/Investors/FIP.html)), and CAMS and KFin share mutual fund and SIP data. SEBI's 2023 consultation named a registered adviser fetching a client's financial data as a natural use case ([SEBI consultation](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/aug-2023/1690887848711.pdf)). Adoption is thin: **only 193 of 9,120 SEBI-regulated entities had joined by December 2024** ([Sahamati](https://sahamati.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Account-Aggregator-Adoption-update-31st-Dec-2024.pdf)). Nivara can reach AA data only by registering, most plausibly as an adviser, or through a regulated partner.

**Data protection and cyber-security deadlines are fixed.** The DPDP Rules were notified in November 2025 as G.S.R. 846(E). Consent Manager provisions started around 13 November 2026, and **most fiduciary duties (notice, security safeguards, breach intimation, retention and children's data) start around 13 May 2027** ([TCSA](https://www.tcsa.in/resources/dpdp-rules-2025-implementation-roadmap); [AMS Shardul](https://www.amsshardul.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Regulatory-Alert-Enforcement-of-DPDP-Act-and-Notification-of-DPDP-Rules.pdf)). Breaches go to the Data Protection Board and affected users without delay, with a detailed report within 72 hours ([AMLegals](https://amlegals.in/dpdp-rules-2025)), and users under 18 need verifiable parental consent ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/top-stories/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-notified-309928)). A one-year log-retention duty and penalties of up to ₹250 crore come from background knowledge and are unverified. CERT-In's 2022 directions already apply to Nivara as a body corporate: **report specified incidents within 6 hours and keep ICT logs for a rolling 180 days, with a copy held in India** ([Khaitan & Co](https://khaitanco.com/sites/default/files/2022-04/Ergo_30042022.pdf); [Lakshmikumaran & Sridharan](https://lakshmisri.com/insights/articles/analyzing-the-new-cert-in-directions-wider-gamut-than-breach-reporting)). SEBI's cyber-security framework would apply only after registration, although brokers will push similar duties down by contract. The CCPA's 2023 dark-patterns guidelines list 13 banned patterns, among them subscription traps, drip pricing and false urgency ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/advertising-marketing-branding/1400996/guidelines-notified-for-prevention-and-regulation-of-dark-patterns)), which rules out pre-ticked consents, hard-to-cancel plans and urgency prompts to switch brokers. No India-specific rule on tax calculators was found, so illustrations should state the assessment year, the Act and rates applied, the simplifications made, and a recommendation to consult a chartered accountant.

## Licensed data costs less than feared at beta scale

**The current data path is not compliant for a public product.** NSE owns its market data and licenses it through NSE Data & Analytics. Its policy bars trading members and subscribers from redistributing market data except as agreed ([NSE data policy](https://www.nseindia.com/static/market-data/nse-data-policy)), and its terms prohibit scraping ([NSE Terms of Use](https://www.nseindia.com/static/nse-terms-of-use)). Showing yfinance prices or prices taken from a user's broker API to other users therefore breaches the terms, and so would scraping exchange filings for fundamentals or announcements.

Compliant display is affordable if it starts delayed. NSE's tariff for 15-minute delayed capital-market data is **about ₹90,000 a year**, the same for any delay of 15 minutes or more ([NSE delayed tariff](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/inline-files/Download%2015%20mins%20delayed%20data%20tariff.pdf)); the sheet is undated, so confirm it against the April 2026 price list. Real-time display is a different scale: the April 2025 domestic tariff showed a ₹24 lakh a year terminal fee and, for free mobile apps, a figure of about ₹22 lakh ([NSE real-time tariff](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/web/sites/default/files/inline-files/Real_Time_Tariff_Domestic_01042025_.pdf)), though the table extract was garbled. A secondary source citing the 2026 list gives ₹29.2 lakh a year per website and ₹875 per user per month for charting applications ([onetradejournal](https://onetradejournal.com/platforms/why-market-data-is-delayed)); both figures are unverified. For fundamentals and corporate actions, CMOTS sells 12-month API baskets with mandatory attribution ([CMOTS terms](https://www.apidatafeed.com/terms-of-use)), and Accord Fintech sells datafeeds alongside a research product that starts at ₹1.25 lakh a year for one user ([Ace Equity Nxt](https://www.aceequitynxt.com/subscribe-now)). Neither publishes API prices, and no source said whether their licences cover LLM summarisation or embeddings. That is a non-display use NSE prices separately, and it must be written into any contract. Mutual fund NAVs are free from AMFI's daily file ([AMFI NAVAll](https://www.amfiindia.com/spages/NAVAll.txt)), although the old format has been discontinued, so the parser needs checking.

**Holdings import changed in September 2025, when AMFI told MF Central to stop sharing portfolio data with more than 100 third-party platforms**, including Groww and Kuvera ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/news/story/why-amfi-blocked-fintechs-access-to-investor-data-a-look-at-the-concerns-and-impact-494695-2025-09-18)). No public MF Central API has replaced it. Four routes remain, each with a different trade-off. CAS PDF upload, parsed locally with the open-source casparser library, covers all mutual funds and demat holdings at no cost and flags schemes that fail to reconcile ([casparser](https://github.com/codereverser/casparser)), but it is manual and periodic. Broker APIs are cheap: **Kite Connect costs ₹500 a month per app key** ([Zerodha support](https://support.zerodha.com/category/trading-and-markets/kite-api/articles/what-are-the-charges-for-kite-apis)), and Angel One and Fyers are reported as free in third-party comparisons ([knowyourbrokerage](https://knowyourbrokerage.in/compare/trading-api-pricing)). Each, however, covers one broker and needs per-user OAuth. The current Groww integration has a structural problem: the Groww Trade API costs ₹499 a month per subscription and its tokens expire daily ([Groww Trade API](https://groww.in/trade-api)), so asking each user to paste a daily token from a personal subscription will not scale. smallcase Gateway offers multi-broker holdings import, including mutual funds, at a quoted price, with known cases where quantity or average price comes back as zero ([smallcase known discrepancy](https://developers.gateway.smallcase.com/page/holdings-import-v2-known-data-discrepancy)). Account Aggregator gives refreshable, consented demat and fund data. Sahamati's standard per-fetch rate card starts on 1 December 2026 ([Elets BFSI](https://bfsi.eletsonline.com/sahamati-defers-account-aggregator-pricing-model-december-1-fiu-fip-fees/)), and depositories return only two years of transactions per fetch ([casparser.in, a vendor source](https://casparser.in/blog/account-aggregator-pricing-2026/)). This favours on-demand refresh over daily sync.

The following cost picture is an estimate assembled from partial tariffs, not from vendor quotes, and every line is **unverified**.

| Phase | Components | Indicative cost (unverified) |
|---|---|---|
| Beta (a few thousand users) | AMFI NAVs; vendor 15-minute delayed or end-of-day display sublicence plus fundamentals basket; self-hosted CAS parsing; multi-user broker OAuth apps | Roughly ₹20,000 to ₹60,000 a month plus GST |
| Account Aggregator (via partner) | 3,000 users, two accounts each, one refresh a month at about ₹10 to ₹30 per fetch | Roughly ₹60,000 to ₹1.8 lakh a month |
| Scale (100,000+ users) | Direct NSE real-time for paying users, licensed news, mutual fund research data | Roughly ₹5 to ₹15 lakh a month or more |

The existing provider interface is the right foundation. It should be extended with separate adapters for licensed market data, AMFI NAVs, fundamentals and each holdings source (CAS, broker OAuth, smallcase, AA). Every record should be tagged with its source, licence and exchange timestamp, and yfinance should be blocked from any production display path.

## The open position is a neutral view of the whole household

An AI chat feature no longer sets Nivara apart. Groww unveiled its GR1 assistant in February 2026 as an opt-in beta with a reported 30,000 to 50,000 testers ([NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/business/groww-introduces-ai-tools-for-retail-investors/story)); whether it is now generally available could not be confirmed. Angel One launched "Ask Angel" for queries and IPO exploration ([Retailintel](https://retailintel.in/signal/angel-one-bets-on-ai-led-personalisation-to-widen-india-s-re-aee96579)). INDmoney presents "mIND" alongside family accounts and automatic import of external funds and stocks ([INDmoney](https://www.indmoney.com/mutual-funds/track-mutual-funds)). Zerodha took a different route and lets users connect Kite to Claude and other assistants through Kite MCP ([TechPP](https://techpp.com/2025/05/21/connect-zerodha-to-claude-ai-using-kite-mcp/)). Tickertape offers a free MCP server as well ([Tapetide](https://tapetide.com/compare/best-ai-stock-analysis-app-india)). Power users can already point a general model at their portfolio, so Nivara has to offer something a raw model connection does not: correct Indian tax and cost arithmetic, a family view, provenance and guardrails.

**The structural gap is neutrality across brokers and asset types.** Broker assistants see only their own platform: GR1 analyses Groww portfolios and watchlists, and Ask Angel centres on support and IPOs. Kuvera's tax harvesting and family accounts cover mutual funds ([Kuvera](https://kuvera.in/blog/tax-harvesting-in-mutual-funds-how-it-works-and-why-it-matters/)), and INDmoney, the closest rival, faces criticism as a distributor whose advice is "not fiduciary in nature" ([Foliyo, competitor-authored](https://foliyo.ai/guides/mf-platforms/indmoney-review/)). Broker concentration is high and shifting. In October 2026 Groww held about 29.4% of active NSE clients, while Angel One overtook a shrinking Zerodha, and the top three together held about 60% ([TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/moodys:41827f63a692b:0-angel-one-overtakes-zerodha-to-become-india-s-second-largest-stockbroker-commands-nearly-15-market-share/)). That churn makes neutral cost comparison useful, provided Nivara takes no undisclosed referral fees, which would also create an association problem for the broker under SEBI's 2024 rules.

The market supports an understanding-first product. NSE passed 13.1 crore unique investors by May 2026 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/market-canvas-widens-nse-s-investor-base-swells-past-26-cr-on-retail-rush-126060800107_1.html)), but only 4.67 crore were active clients in October 2026, so many investors are dormant or occasional. SIP inflows have run above ₹32,000 crore a month for three consecutive months ([Kotak Neo](https://www.kotakneo.com/news/mutual-funds/equity-mutual-fund-inflows-fall-14-percent-october-2026-sip-contributions/)). SEBI's 2025 survey of 91,950 households found that **63% know of at least one securities product, but only 9.5% invest and just 36% of investors have moderate or high knowledge** ([SEBI Investor Survey 2025](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/commondocs/jan-2026/Investor%20Survey%202025%20Main%20Report.pdf)). A widely quoted figure that 62% of investors are swayed by finfluencers rests on a single outlet ([Moneylife MAS360](https://mas360.moneylife.in/article/sebi-survey-reveals-indian-investors-prefer-social-media-influencers-over-traditional-financial-advisors/4832.html)) and should be treated as unverified. Consumer guides tell investors to prefer official routes such as CAS and Account Aggregator, to avoid apps that ask for passwords, and to ask how a free tracker earns money ([GetBelong](https://getbelong.com/blog/portfolio-tracker-apps-in-india/)). Nivara's best position therefore combines five things: a cross-broker, cross-asset household view; tax and cost illustrations that span brokers and funds; neutral broker-cost comparison; EPF and NPS coverage where incumbents are weak or manual; and an openly non-commission business model.

Pricing evidence points to modest subscriptions. Screener's AI reportedly costs about ₹10 to ₹30 an answer, and Trendlyne's AI plan about ₹390 a month ([Tapetide](https://tapetide.com/compare/best-ai-stock-analysis-app-india)). Globally, AI is bundled with brokerage or with premium tiers such as Robinhood Gold at $5 a month ([Walnut](https://walnutinvest.com/resources/which-brokers-have-an-ai-assistant)). A freemium tier with a capped number of Nia questions, and a premium plan somewhere between ₹100 and ₹400 a month, fits these reference points. The range is an inference, and no willingness-to-pay data was found. B2B licensing of the explanation layer to registered advisers or smaller brokers is a credible second line that also keeps incentives clean. Evidence was missing on several competitors (Paytm Money, ET Money, smallcase, Dezerv) and on vernacular support in rival assistants, so the vernacular opportunity is plausible but unproven.

## Nia should narrate numbers that code computes

The backend Nia route is a reasonable start. It keeps chat history on the server, rejects history supplied by the client, takes its model from config and maps provider errors to proper status codes (`backend/app/routers/ai_chat.py:45-87`). But it amounts to holdings pasted into a system prompt, with a prompt-level instruction not to advise and no check on the output. It runs synchronous database queries inside an async handler, creates a new HTTP client per request, crashes on non-JSON provider output, hard-codes the 2023 API version header, and has no per-user quotas, cost telemetry or way to delete history. These defects are fixable. The larger need is a different shape.

**The recommended design is a single model in a tool loop, where every number comes from a deterministic service.** Nivara's existing calculators become strictly typed tools, such as portfolio valuation, XIRR, capital-gains estimate, goal projection, price history, filing search and news search. Anthropic's strict tool use validates tool names and inputs against a schema, and its JSON outputs mode constrains the final answer to a schema with "no retries needed" ([Anthropic structured outputs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs)). For interface cards, the model should return references to tool-output IDs, and the frontend should render the actual figure from the tool result rather than from model text. Retrieved filings and licensed news should go in as `search_result` blocks, which give native citations to Nivara's own content without prompt-engineered citation formats ([Anthropic search results](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/search-results)). Whether citations and JSON output can be combined in one response was not confirmed. Robinhood's engineering team reportedly moved from many narrow agents to "one capable model in a loop" because shared context such as holdings was needed everywhere ([Robinhood blog](https://robinhood.com/us/en/careers/blog/loop-stays-dumb-so-the-model-can-be-smart/)); this is known only from a search snippet. Morgan Stanley's published approach was to build an evaluation framework for each use case before launch ([OpenAI case study](https://openai.com/index/morgan-stanley/)), a vendor-published but sensible precedent.

The case for keeping arithmetic out of the model is empirical. **Frontier models still show 10% to 20% error rates on multi-step numerical reasoning over financial reports** ([FAITH, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05201v1)), and a 2026 study found a leading model vulnerable to injected fake news 45% of the time ([arXiv 2610.26438](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26438)), a figure taken from a snippet. Nia's guardrails should therefore work in layers. A small, fast model classifies intent, routing "should I buy X?" to a helpful reframe that explains the factors a person would weigh and links to SEBI's register of advisers, rather than a cold refusal. A post-generation check catches imperative trading verbs, target prices and return claims. A numeric check extracts every figure in the answer and confirms each matches a tool output or a cited document span, regenerating the answer if any does not. Retrieved text enters only as tool or search content, stripped of hidden markup, and Nia has no side-effecting tools, so a successful injection can at worst mislead an explanation. Only ISIN, quantity and cost should be sent to the model, never PAN, names, folio or demat numbers, under a processor agreement that DPDP will require. Every turn should log the prompt version, pinned model ID, tool calls, sources, guardrail verdicts and final text, which matches SEBI's draft emphasis on model governance.

Cost is not the constraint. The current models list Haiku 5.5 from $0.10 per million input tokens, Sonnet 5.5 at $2 and Opus 5.5 at $4, with prompt-cache reads at 5% to 10% of input price and 50% off through the Batch API ([Anthropic models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/models/overview)). A sensible split uses Haiku for classification and guardrails, Sonnet as the default answerer and Opus only for long multi-document research, with the system prompt and tool definitions cached and weekly portfolio digests run in batch. The evaluation set is the asset that makes this safe to change. It should be a version-controlled set run in CI on every prompt or model change, covering synthetic portfolios with known answers, questions on Nifty company filings with gold citations, advice-bait prompts in English, Hindi and Hinglish, and poisoned news items. Checking citation accuracy matters as much as checking that citations are present, because **users trusted answers more when citations were attached even when the citations were random** ([Ding et al., AAAI 2025](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/34550); [NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/explainable-ai/)). Traces should follow the OpenTelemetry GenAI conventions, which are still in development status ([OpenTelemetry](https://opentelemetry.io/blog/2026/genai-observability/)), so they can feed a self-hosted Langfuse instance (which helps with data residency) or Datadog.

The interface should serve investors who are new and often under-informed. National financial literacy was about 24% to 27% in the last NCFE survey, from 2019 ([NCAER](https://ncaer.org/?p=28914)). SEBI found **about 91% of individual F&O traders lost money in FY25, with net losses of about ₹1.06 lakh crore** ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/net-losses-of-traders-in-fo-widens-in-fy25-sebi-study-125070701221_1.html)), and heavy trading has long been linked to worse returns ([Barber and Odean](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/returns/returns.html)). Every number should carry an "as of" time and source. A "How Nia got this" expander should list the calculations performed. A short, persistent "Nia explains; it does not advise" badge and an onboarding card that states the assistant's limits should replace walls of disclaimers. Metrics such as XIRR, expense ratio and LTCG should come with plain-language glosses and examples drawn from the user's own data. Nudges should be computed deterministically and explained by the model: concentration and fund overlap, cost drag, drawdown context during falls, and neutral, cited SEBI loss statistics when derivatives appear. Weekly or monthly digests and goal-progress notices are better than price pushes, and success should be measured by understanding and SIP persistence rather than trade count. Claude can answer directly in Hindi or Hinglish, while Indian-system number formatting (lakh and crore) stays in code. Sarvam's 2026 speech models, Saaras v3 for speech-to-text and Bulbul for text-to-speech in up to 22 languages ([Sarvam changelog](https://docs.sarvam.ai/api/changelog)), make a vernacular voice mode practical. Their benchmark claims are vendor-reported.

## A prioritised roadmap from prototype to credible beta

The order below follows dependencies. Defects that damage trust come first, because they are cheap and visible. The transaction model comes next, because tax, XIRR, imports and reconciliation all depend on it. Nia's expansion follows once the numbers beneath it are right. Regulatory work runs in parallel from the start, because it sets the limits for everything else. The repository contains no team or capacity information, so the timings are judgements, not estimates.

| Priority and timing | Work | Why now |
|---|---|---|
| P0, first two weeks | Restore CI (fix the two lint errors, upgrade vite, add Dependabot); lowercase emails on register and login with a unique index; reject known template secrets and require high-entropy keys; bound `X-Request-ID` to 64 characters; correct the stale README and assessment claims | Verified defects that lock users out, allow token forgery or break the build |
| P0, first two weeks | Tax: loss set-off, 4% cess, strict date parsing with no 400-day default, ETF classification by underlying, Income-tax Act 2025 references. Charges: trade side, futures and options split with premium input, 2026 STT rates, segment-specific exchange charges, SEBI fees in the GST base | Headline figures are currently wrong while appearing precise to the paisa |
| P0, start immediately | Written opinion from securities and data-protection counsel on Nia's scope, the three-month price-data rule and the registration route; choose a licence for the code | Every later product decision depends on where the advice line falls |
| P1, months 1 to 3 | Transaction-based schema (instruments keyed by ISIN, transactions, lots, corporate actions); CAS upload via casparser; persist and reconcile broker snapshots; encrypted token store with consent records and revocation; Motilal callback with `state` | The keystone for XIRR, tax lots, reconciliation and multi-source import |
| P1, months 1 to 3 | Sign a vendor delayed or end-of-day display licence and fundamentals basket, checking non-display and LLM use; AMFI NAV adapter; Redis TTL cache; batched, async quotes; honour the stored exchange and exchange timestamps; block yfinance from production | Removes the licensing breach and the per-holding blocking calls |
| P1, months 1 to 3 | Security hardening: httpOnly cookies with CSRF protection or a strict SPA CSP and HSTS; token revocation and refresh; per-account login throttle; Redis rate limiter keyed on user and trusted proxy IP, with per-endpoint limits; audit all financial mutations | Closes the remaining account-takeover and abuse paths |
| P1, months 1 to 3 | Wire the frontend to the existing portfolio and market APIs, replace demo holdings and scripted Nia, add routing, error and stale-data states, and Vitest and Playwright tests | Turns the shell into a usable product |
| P2, months 3 to 6 | Nia as a tool loop over deterministic services, with strict schemas, `search_result` citations, intent pre-classifier, advice and numeric post-checks, PII minimisation, per-user quotas, history deletion and per-turn audit logs; India-specific eval set in CI | Delivers grounded explanation without drifting into advice |

| P2, months 3 to 6 | Household and family views; neutral broker-cost comparison with disclosed or no referrals; deterministic nudges and weekly digests; Hindi and Hinglish answers | The features that set Nivara apart from broker assistants |
| P3, months 6 to 12 | DPDP readiness before May 2027 (itemised consent notice, easy withdrawal, retention schedule, age gate, processor contracts, one incident runbook covering CERT-In 6 hours and DPDP 72 hours, 180-day logs in India); multi-user broker OAuth apps or smallcase Gateway; AA via a regulated partner or own registration; EPF and NPS coverage; premium tier and voice mode | Fixed legal deadlines, plus the scalable data channels and revenue |

## Conclusion

Nivara's position in the market is also its main risk. The feature that sets it apart from broker assistants, an AI that looks across a person's whole household of holdings, is exactly the behaviour SEBI's substance-over-label test examines most closely. Account Aggregator, the cleanest data channel, opens only to regulated entities. The two choices are therefore linked: registering as an adviser, or partnering with one, would both make personalised features lawful and unlock consented data. That may be worth pursuing sooner than the prototype stage suggests, even though it brings AI-disclosure and accountability duties. A decision to stay unregistered is coherent but narrower. In that case Nia must be built as a narrator of deterministic facts with tested refusal paths, and the commercial case rests on CAS import, broker OAuth and a subscription priced like a research tool.

The more immediate lesson is that this product's trust rests on its arithmetic and its honesty about state, and both are currently weaker than its documents claim. A tax figure that ignores losses, prices taken from an unlicensed source, and documentation that reports checks as passing when they now fail all work against the "never fabricate" promise more than any missing feature does. Fixing them is cheap, and a working evaluation and audit trail for Nia will be the evidence that brokers, a partner adviser and, eventually, SEBI's final AI guidelines will ask for.
